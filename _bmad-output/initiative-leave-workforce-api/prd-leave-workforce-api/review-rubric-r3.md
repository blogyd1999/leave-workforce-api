# PRD Quality Review — Leave and Workforce Management API (r3)

## Overall verdict
This is a strong, unusually testable PRD. It has a clear thesis (one narrow, rule-enforcing leave path with a two-person control on the most powerful actions), numeric thresholds almost everywhere, explicit non-goals and limitations, and a mostly honest approved/unapproved split in §10. The risk is concentrated in the new credential and privileged-action machinery (FR-2, FR-27 to FR-31, SEC-11, SEC-22 to SEC-24). There the PRD contains one reachability dead end (no way to ever get a second Administrator), one account-state dead end (a used Temporary Password whose session lapsed), and a few places where approved rows (RV-4, RV-5, SEC-12) quietly carry content that is still only proposed (D8, Q-E3). Draft status was not counted as a defect. Fixing the two high findings and the approved/unapproved leakage would make it ready to hand to architecture.

## Decision-readiness — adequate
§10.4 is a good model of honest decision surfacing. Each Q-E item states what the PRD currently says, why it needs approval and, for Q-E1, Q-E4 and Q-E8, the alternative. Trade-offs are named: L-6 admits "an operational burden accepted for the MVP", and L-9 admits collusion and lockout abuse. Q-E8 is a genuine open question.

The weakness is the boundary between approved and proposed.
- RV-4 (approved) says "Request span at most 366 days, start at most 2 years ahead, balance values bounded". Q-D D8 lists the same numbers as defaults still awaiting confirmation, and FR-14 and FR-11 tag them `[ASSUMPTION: indicative]`.
- RV-5 (approved) cites UJ-8 in "Where applied". UJ-8 is the role-grant journey, and granting is the Q-E3 addition, not part of what RV-5 approved ("creating or resetting").
- SEC-12 states "Creating or granting an Administrator, and resetting an Administrator's password, need a second Administrator" as flat fact, with no tag.
- Q-E1, Q-E3 and Q-E5 shape the whole credential model (UJ-1, 3, 6, 7, FR-2, FR-7, L-6, SM-7). The inline `[ASSUMPTION: Q-E…]` tag appears only on one clause each: FR-30's first line, FR-31's expiry bullet and FR-3's clearing bullet. A reader extracting FR-31's intro, FR-1, FR-5 or UJ-8 cannot tell they depend on an unapproved proposal.

### Findings
- **medium** Approved row RV-4 contains content that Q-D D8 still lists as unconfirmed (§10.1 RV-4 vs §10.4 D8, FR-14, FR-11, FR-12) — the same numeric bounds (366 days, 2 years, 1000) appear as approved and as awaiting confirmation. *Fix:* split RV-4 into the approved principle (bounded) and the proposed numbers (D8), or move D8 into §10.1 if the owner approved the values.
- **medium** RV-5 "Where applied" cites UJ-8 and SEC-12 presents granting as settled, though granting is only Q-E3 (§10.1 RV-5, §5 SEC-12, §2.3 UJ-8, §4.1 §Description which omits granting, US-19, SM-6) — an unapproved proposal reads as approved. *Fix:* tag the "grant" scope with `[ASSUMPTION: Q-E3]` wherever it appears (FR-5, FR-31 intro, SEC-12, UJ-8, US-19), or narrow RV-5's Where-applied to FR-31's create/reset scope.
- **medium** Tag coverage of Q-E proposals is partial (FR-2 and FR-30 24 hours, SEC-22 "default 24 hours" and Glossary "Temporary Password" carry no Q-E5 tag; L-10 and FR-19 carry no Q-E8 tag; SEC-5 tags "indicative values" without Q-E7). *Fix:* add the Q-E id to each inline tag.
- **low** The Q-E and §10.3 tables speak in conversational voice ("Your instruction says", "the earlier review") and cite AP/RV ids whose source reviews are not part of the PRD (§10.1, §10.3, §10.4). Pulled out alone, the "why" column is not self-contained. *Fix:* restate the instruction being interpreted in one neutral sentence.

## Substance over theater — strong
There is no vision theater: the Vision names the product-specific bets (no overlap or overspend by construction, two-person control). NFRs carry thresholds: 15 min tokens, 12 to 128 character passwords, 500 ms p95 at 200 employees, page size 25/100, back-off 5/1 min/15 min. The six numbered limitations L-1 to L-11 are specific and honest. SEC-23 and FR-25 reflect real design thinking, not boilerplate. The five "personas" in §2.1 include a "Front-end developer (secondary)" who drives only SM-9 and the OpenAPI requirement, which is thin but not harmful. Open-items density (8 Q-E items, 16 D defaults, 4 recommended defaults, about 20 assumptions) is high for a PRD that feeds architecture, but it is declared and labelled blocking versus non-blocking.

### Findings
- **low** Five personas (Employee, Manager, Administrator, Operator, Front-end developer) is one above the usual ceiling (§2.1). The front-end developer persona drives no FR. *Fix:* keep as a note under SM-9 rather than a persona, or accept as is.

## Strategic coherence — strong
The thesis is stated in §1 and every feature serves it: reserve-then-convert balance, one decider, audit trail, two-person control. Scope kind is problem-solving and the exclusions in §7 match it. SM-1 to SM-7 validate the rules rather than activity, and SM-C1 to SM-C3 are real counter-metrics tied to specific SMs. SMs are test-shaped, which fits a learning project with no live users. SM-2's invariants list (Reserved equals sum of Pending, no overlap, distinct Initiator and Approver) is a good example of a metric that tests the thesis. §8.3 increment ordering is justified by dependencies, not ease.

### Findings
- **low** SM-1 "Validates" omits FR-18 and FR-21, which UJ-2 needs, and FR-12. SM-2 omits FR-5 (mutual role removal, named in §6) and FR-16 (approve versus cancel, named in §6). *Fix:* add them.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate
Most FRs have crisp, testable consequences with explicit codes (FR-1, 3, 4, 12, 14, 19 to 21, 31). Concurrency outcomes are specified, with "succeed once; loser receives 409". The weak spots are state-machine gaps and unspecified codes, not adjectives. Residual vague words: "short grace window" (FR-8, SEC-4), "a clear refusal" (FR-27), "bounded failure count" (FR-25), and "a stable error code" (FR-2).

### Findings
- **high** No path to a second Administrator (FR-27, FR-29, FR-31, UJ-6 to UJ-8, SM-1). FR-27 succeeds "only when no Administrator Account exists" and so creates exactly one Administrator. Granting or creating an Administrator needs a different Administrator (FR-31). FR-29 only resets access for an existing Administrator, yet FR-31's last bullet and FR-29 itself call it "the only sanctioned alternative" and "the only sanctioned way around FR-31". No sanctioned route exists from UJ-6's end state (one Administrator) to UJ-8's entry state (two). The headline two-person control cannot be exercised, the "last active Administrator" rules in FR-5 can never be tested at 2, and SM-1 and SM-6 depend on fixtures that no FR provides. *Fix:* decide how the second Administrator comes to exist (for example, the Bootstrap Operation or a variant of FR-29 may create or grant a further Administrator, audited, with a reason, or bootstrap creates two), state it as an FR, and correct the "only sanctioned alternative" wording.
- **high** Temporary Password and Must-Change dead end (FR-2, FR-30, FR-7, Glossary "Awaiting Password" and "Must-Change State"). A Temporary Password "works for one login only". After it is used the Account is in the Must-Change State, but FR-2 says the session "ends no later than the Temporary Password's original expiry". If the user logs in and does not change the password in time, the password is spent, so login returns 401. The Account is not Awaiting Password, so FR-30 ("for an Account that is Awaiting Password") does not allow re-issue. No transition from Must-Change State back to Awaiting Password is defined. An ordinary Account can be rescued only by an Administrator reset (FR-28). An Administrator Account needs two Administrators or FR-29. The same gap applies to a Temporary Password that expires unused (is the state then still "Awaiting Password"?). *Fix:* define the Account state machine (Awaiting Password, Must-Change, Active, Deactivated) with transitions, including "Must-Change expiry returns to Awaiting Password", and let FR-30 apply to that state.
- **medium** Must-Change State and token refresh contradict (FR-2, SEC-11 rule 3, SEC-7). FR-2 says an Account in the Must-Change State "can call only change-password and logout" (403 otherwise), but also that expiry is "re-checked on every call … including a token refresh". Refresh is neither allowed in the list nor 403. *Fix:* decide whether refresh is allowed in Must-Change; if so add it to the allowed set in FR-2 and SEC-11 rule 3, otherwise delete "including a token refresh".
- **medium** FR-31 defines the control but not its operations (FR-31, UJ-8, US-19). There is no operation to list or read pending Privileged Requests (the Approver "reviews it"), no response codes for a non-Administrator touching one (403) or an unknown id (404), and no definition of what "resetting an Administrator's password" executes. FR-28 forbids Administrators from using the reset on any Administrator, and its effects (Awaiting Password, sessions ended, throttle cleared) are defined only for the ordinary path. The Q-E2 "own Direct Manager" restriction is not stated for the privileged reset. *Fix:* add the read/list/approve/reject behaviour and say that an approved reset applies FR-28's effects.
- **medium** FR-1 cannot create an Administrator as written (FR-1, FR-31, FR-5). FR-1's inputs are "name, unique work email and an optional Direct Manager", with no role. Yet it says creating an Account "that has the Administrator role is a Privileged Administrator Action". Q-E3 itself says the control would otherwise be bypassed by create-then-grant, so the create-as-Administrator path is redundant with FR-5 grant. *Fix:* either add a role input to FR-1 or drop "create with the Administrator role" from FR-1, FR-31, SEC-12 and UJ-8 context, keeping grant only.
- **medium** Response codes for out-of-bound inputs and a FR-14 vs SEC-11 conflict (FR-14, SEC-11 rule 7, D15). FR-14 gives 400 for "First Working Day not before Today", but SEC-11 rule 7 lists "First Working Day passed" among 409 business-rule refusals (this meant FR-19, but the wording collides). The 366-day span, 2-year horizon, 500-character note, FR-1 name and email limits and FR-20 reason limit have no stated code (rule 6 implies 400). A weekend-only range has no "First Working Day" (Glossary), so the order of the 400 check and the 409 weekend check is undefined. *Fix:* state in rule 7 "(approval only)" for First Working Day and give an explicit code or "400" to each bound bullet.
- **medium** FR-18 and FR-23 do not say the Manager sees which Employee a request belongs to (FR-18 "dates, Requested Days, note and the Employee's Available days"; FR-23). UJ-2 needs it, and FR-6 supplies only the Direct Report list separately. *Fix:* add "Employee name" to each item.
- **medium** Audit class of several events is ambiguous (Glossary "Security-Class Event", FR-25, L-11, FR-32). Security-Class covers "balance" events, but it is unclear whether a Leave Request approval, which reduces Total, is Security-Class or Ordinary. Also unclear are login success, lockout transitions, refresh-token reuse, Administrator reads and audit queries. L-11 says "balance … events are kept for the longer Security Retention Period", which the FR-25 list does not guarantee for approvals. *Fix:* add a classification column or list to FR-25.
- **medium** A single Administrator can deactivate or strip the role of another Administrator (FR-4, FR-5, US-19, L-9). Only grant, create and reset are two-person. The US-19 "so that no single Administrator can create or take over another" is met, but unilateral removal or lockout of a peer is not named as a limitation (§6 lists only "two Administrators removing each other's role" as a race). *Fix:* add to L-9 or make removal and deactivation of an Administrator Privileged.
- **low** FR-2 "must differ from both the Temporary Password and the current password" is redundant, since the Temporary Password is the current password in Must-Change State.
- **low** FR-3 gives no code for "An Employee cannot be their own Direct Manager" when the actor is an Administrator acting on someone else. The 403 applies only to an Administrator's own Account.
- **low** Small unbounded edge cases: FR-8 "short grace window" has no bound (even as an assumption), FR-11's repeat set-opening-balance attempt has no stated code, FR-28 on a deactivated target is unspecified, and whether failures against an Awaiting Password Account count toward SEC-5 is unspecified.
- **low** FR-17 lists "Decider" in the response but also says "the Direct Manager's name and no other manager data". The Decider may be a former manager. FR-22's status history also exposes "actors", including Administrators for cancellations by FR-3 or FR-4. *Fix:* say what actor identity an Employee sees.

## Scope honesty — strong
§7 and §8.2 are explicit and tie to limitations (L-1, L-3, L-8, L-10). Silent assumptions are mostly tagged, and de-scoping is honestly proposed (Q-E4, Q-E8 alternatives). The PRD states in §10.4 "No unapproved item is being treated as approved", which is true for most but not all content (see Decision-readiness). Open-items density is high, but the PRD labels what blocks finalization (Q-E) versus not.

### Findings
- **medium** FR-27, FR-29 and FR-30 repeat "not available to anonymous callers over the public API" (also Glossary "Operator Operation — run outside the public API"). The phrasing implies the operations might be available to authenticated callers or to anonymous ones before initialization. FR-27's "After initialization, the operation is not available to anonymous callers over the public API" especially implies it could be a public endpoint beforehand, which contradicts the Glossary. *Fix:* say "is not exposed over the public API at all", or state that before initialization the endpoint exists and is secured by a deployment secret.

## Downstream usability — adequate
IDs are contiguous (FR-1 to FR-32, with FR-27 to FR-32 placed out of order and §4.8 explicitly says so). The SEC, SM, US, UJ, AP-1 to AP-9, RV-1 to RV-9, Q-E1 to Q-E8, D1 to D16 and L-1 to L-11 sets all resolve, with no gaps or duplicates. The Glossary is thorough, and UJ protagonists are named with inline context. The Mechanical notes below list what does not round-trip.

### Findings
- **medium** The glossary definitions of Manager and Direct Manager are circular and active-only (Glossary "Manager", "Direct Manager", FR-3, FR-6, FR-23). "Manager — an Employee who is the Direct Manager of at least one active Employee" and "Direct Manager — the single Manager assigned". Under the first, an Employee assigned to a person with no active reports has no valid Direct Manager. FR-6 and FR-23 give Managers deactivated Direct Reports in their lists and history, but a Manager whose reports are all deactivated is no longer a "Manager". *Fix:* define Direct Manager as the Employee assigned (any active Account) and Manager capability as having any Direct Report (active or not) for read purposes.
- **low** Glossary drift: "Account … has an active or deactivated state" but Awaiting Password and Must-Change State are also Account states (RV-3 says "must-change flag", SEC-11 "Must-Change-State"); "credentials" (§8.3 item 1, D1, Q-E1 title) versus "password" (FR-1 says "no password and no credential of any kind"); lower-case "operator operation", "operator", "temporary password" in UJ-1, UJ-3, UJ-6; "Bootstrap Operation" (UJ-6, FR-27) and "Privileged Administrator Action" (FR-1, FR-31, §6) used as terms but not defined in the Glossary; "per account" in SEC-5.
- **low** UJ-7's persona line names only Dev, while Meera and Karan appear without context, and its edge case sends the reader to "(UJ-8)" for Administrator resets, though UJ-8 shows only a role grant.
- **low** FR-14 says "whether that is a Top-Most Person or an Employee not yet assigned a manager", but the Glossary defines Top-Most Person as any Employee with no Direct Manager, which already includes the unassigned.
- **low** US coverage: FR-5 (manage the Administrator role) maps to no user story, and FR-2 maps only to US-1 although US-5 is the login story.
- **low** §10.1 "Where applied" is accurate overall (checked each row). Minor omissions or looseness: AP-1 lists FR-6, which gives only 403 and no concealment 404; RV-2 omits FR-31; RV-3 omits FR-2 and SEC-24; RV-5 omits FR-1 and FR-5, where the two-person rule also applies.

## Shape fit — adequate
The PRD is capability-spec shaped with eight UJs, 21 user stories and 32 FRs. For an internal-tool-shaped API that is somewhat over-formalized: the user-story table restates the FRs (§4.8 admits "Acceptance criteria … are the testable consequences of the FRs"). The UJs are not furniture, though, since each carries a real edge-case rule and they feed SM-1. The Operator as a non-Account actor is modelled cleanly, and the 400/401/403/404/409 policy in SEC-11 is the right level of detail for a security-sensitive API PRD.

### Findings
- **low** The US table is a thin duplicate of FR content and costs maintenance; consider keeping it only as a ticketing index (§4.8).
